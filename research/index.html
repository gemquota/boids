ATLAS / OVERVIEW
00 / EXECUTIVE SYNTHESIS

From flocking rules to an evolving ecosystem

The strongest direction is not to make every agent smarter by adding more arbitrary steering weights. It is to build a coherent causal system in which local perception, physical constraints, energy budgets, heritable variation and environmental feedback jointly determine behaviour.

5

Coupled research domains

Collective motion, mechanics, ecology, evolution and neural control must be designed as one system, not five disconnected feature piles.

4

Levels of explanation

Agent mechanisms → local interactions → population dynamics → evolutionary change across generations.

1

Shared population

Predatory behaviour is a conditional strategy expressed by individuals in the same evolving population, not a hard-coded second species.

0

Free biological realism

Visual plausibility is not validation. Units, causal mechanisms, controls and uncertainty need explicit treatment.

Primary conclusions

1. Preserve local interaction, but make perception explicit
Reynolds' model demonstrates that coordinated motion can emerge from decentralised agents with limited local information. Perception radius, field of view, occlusion, sensory noise and interaction zones are not cosmetic controls: they define the information available to an agent. The simulation should avoid silently giving every boid global knowledge.
2. Separate physical state from behavioural intent
A controller should request steering or acceleration; the motion integrator should apply bounded acceleration, speed limits, drag and collision constraints. This prevents neural outputs from directly teleporting position or bypassing physical costs. Use fixed simulation steps and render interpolation.
3. Make predation an evolved behavioural strategy
Predatory success should depend on traits and context: sensory acuity, aggression, pursuit persistence, body size, speed, energy reserves, risk tolerance, kin recognition, group coordination and prey vulnerability. A single species label should not dictate the role. The same individual can forage, scavenge, flee or hunt depending on state and opportunities.
4. Couple ecology to evolutionary fitness through actual events
Energy acquisition, metabolic costs, injury, starvation, reproductive investment and offspring survival should produce fitness differences. Avoid a detached fitness score that rewards arbitrary proxy values without affecting lifetime reproductive success.
5. Treat complexity as a hypothesis to measure
Flocks, morphs, strategies and trophic roles should be detected from observed trajectories and heritable traits, not assigned by visual colour or hard-coded categories. Repeat runs with controlled seeds and compare distributions, not one attractive screenshot.
Design principle: evolve parameters that control mechanisms, not labels that preordain outcomes. A “hunter” is an observed behavioural phenotype, not a species flag.

Research quality labels

ESTABLISHED directly supported by a cited paper, review or documented method. ENGINEERING practical implementation guidance inferred from numerical/computational literature. HYPOTHESIS proposed experiment requiring validation in this simulation.

01 / COLLECTIVE MOTION

Flocking, schooling and local coordination

A flock is not a shape that agents are instructed to maintain. It is a dynamic pattern generated by local interactions, limited perception, inertia and environmental context.

01.1 Reynolds' three-rule model

Reynolds (1987) described independent actors whose aggregate motion emerges from local perception, simulated physics and steering behaviours. The familiar operational decomposition is separation, alignment and cohesion. The original paper also discusses obstacle avoidance, goal seeking, speed limits and momentum; reducing it to three weighted vectors misses much of the model's character.

RuleLocal signalTypical failure if over-weightedUseful diagnostics
SeparationRepulsion from nearby neighbours, often distance weighted.Fragmentation, jitter, isolated agents.Nearest-neighbour distance, collision rate.
AlignmentAverage heading or velocity of visible neighbours.Rigid synchrony, waves, over-coordination.Polarisation, heading variance.
CohesionSteering toward local neighbour centroid.Clumping, oscillation, crowding.Cluster size, local density, radius of gyration.
aᵢ = clamp( wₛ Sᵢ + wₐ Aᵢ + w꜀ Cᵢ + wₒ Oᵢ + wₑ Eᵢ , aₘₐₓ )

This is a useful engineering abstraction, not a universal biological law. Normalisation, priority ordering, steering limits and behavioural state alter the result.

01.2 Perception and neighbourhood topology

Distance and field of view
Use a finite perception radius and a forward-facing field-of-view cone. A rear blind zone makes turning and pursuit meaningfully directional. Test whether field of view is measured in degrees, cosine threshold or angular sectors, and keep the convention documented.
Interaction zones
A repulsion zone can be nested inside orientation and attraction zones. A three-zone model can create distinct short-range avoidance, intermediate alignment and longer-range cohesion. Overlap and priority rules matter: simultaneous weighted forces can cancel in unintuitive ways.
Occlusion and sensory uncertainty
Line-of-sight occlusion, limited neighbour sampling, delayed sensing and noise can produce more realistic uncertainty and reduce hidden global coordination. These are costly; add them behind explicit fidelity settings and measure their impact.

01.3 Useful extensions

Goal seeking & migration

External targets or gradients add direction without replacing local flock rules. Use weighted goal attraction and route persistence.

Obstacle avoidance

Reynolds' steer-to-avoid method anticipates forward collision and steers around a silhouette, rather than merely pushing away from all nearby geometry.

Collective transitions

Density, polarisation, neighbour count and noise can move populations between milling, schooling, dispersal and coherent travel.

Heterogeneous interaction

Individuals can differ in perception, preferred spacing, social attraction and responsiveness. Heterogeneity can destabilise or diversify collective states.

01.4 Failure modes & testable fixes

SymptomLikely mechanismTest
Oscillatory turningExcess steering gain, conflicting vectors, no damping.Reduce acceleration authority; inspect each force separately.
Rigid clumpingCohesion too strong, weak separation, overlong range.Plot neighbour distance distributions and zone occupancy.
Flock dissolvesSeparation or noise dominates; alignment weak.Map outcomes across weight ratios and perception radius.

Recommendation: add interaction-zone and perception experiments before adding another dozen steering multipliers. More knobs do not repair a poorly defined local information model.
02 / PHYSICAL SIMULATION

Motion, collisions, numerical stability and water

The physics layer is the contract between what an agent wants to do and what the world permits it to do. It should be deterministic enough to test, stable over long runs and cheap enough for mobile hardware.

02.1 Time integration and motion state

Use a fixed simulation timestep independent of display refresh. Accumulate wall-clock time, step the simulation at a stable interval, cap catch-up work, and interpolate render state. Variable large timesteps make collision, neural update and energy calculations depend on frame rate.

MethodStrengthTrade-offRole here
Explicit EulerSimple and cheap.Can exhibit energy drift and poor stability.Teaching baseline only.
Semi-implicit EulerVelocity updated before position; robust for many game-like force systems.First-order accuracy; timestep still matters.Practical default for agent motion.
Velocity VerletSecond-order, useful long-term behaviour for conservative dynamics.Requires force evaluation at new position; less natural for arbitrary steering.Candidate for physical submodels.
RK4High local accuracy for smooth ODEs.Multiple force evaluations; not automatically energy preserving.Reference tests, not necessarily real-time default.
Implicit methodsCan handle stiff systems.Requires solving equations; more implementation complexity.Only if stiff constraints demand it.
vₜ₊₁ = vₜ + aₜ Δt
xₜ₊₁ = xₜ + vₜ₊₁ Δt

02.2 Neighbour search and broad phase

Naive all-pairs neighbourhood work scales as O(N²). Spatial subdivision limits exact checks to nearby cells. Uniform grids/spatial hashes are a strong fit when interaction radii are similar and the world is large relative to cell size. A cell width near the maximum query radius is a practical starting point, but exact choice depends on density, movement and query distribution.

Spatial hash pipeline
  1. Map each agent position to an integer cell coordinate.
  2. Hash or sort cell keys and build contiguous cell ranges.
  3. For each agent, inspect its own and adjacent cells sufficient to cover the query radius.
  4. Apply exact distance and perception tests to candidates.
  5. Reuse the index for flock, food and predator queries where semantics permit.

02.3 Contacts, boundaries and constraints

Separate broad-phase candidate generation from narrow-phase contact resolution. For circles, exact overlap tests are cheap; for obstacles, use geometry appropriate to the shape. Use continuous/swept collision tests or substeps when displacement per step approaches object diameter. Boundary wrapping and reflecting walls are different world topologies and should not be conflated.

Periodic world

Minimum-image distances are needed across wrap boundaries; otherwise neighbours on opposite edges appear far apart despite being adjacent.

Reflective world

Resolve contact normal velocity and apply restitution/friction consistently. A simple direction flip can inject energy if used carelessly.

02.4 Fluids, currents and hydrodynamic coupling

Do not treat “water” as one undifferentiated noise field. A layered approximation can deliver meaningful behaviour at different costs.

LayerModelWhat it givesCost / caveat
0Analytic current fieldCoherent drift, gradients, vortices.Cheap; no fluid feedback.
1Grid-based shallow-water / velocity fieldAdvection and evolving flow.Moderate; numerical diffusion and boundary conditions matter.
2SPH particlesLagrangian free surfaces and particle-fluid coupling.Neighbour search, pressure stability, density errors and timestep constraints.
3Full CFD / multiphysicsMore complete fluid dynamics.Far beyond a lightweight browser ecosystem's baseline budget.
SPH essentials
Smoothed Particle Hydrodynamics estimates continuum quantities from neighbouring particles using a smoothing kernel. Density, pressure and viscosity are reconstructed locally. It is meshless and useful for free surfaces and fluid-structure interaction, but accuracy and stability depend on formulation, kernel, boundary treatment, particle disorder and timestep. A CFL-style restriction limits how far information travels relative to kernel support during one step. Avoid claiming that “SPH” alone guarantees realistic water.
Recommended path: first build a coherent analytic/grid current field and couple boids through drag, buoyancy-like vertical bias, flow alignment and energy costs. Add SPH only as a separately benchmarked subsystem if fluid-agent interaction is a core research question.
03 / ECOLOGY & LIFE HISTORY

Energy, resource competition and trophic roles

An ecosystem becomes interesting when survival, reproduction and behaviour compete for limited resources. A coherent energy economy is more valuable than a large number of disconnected “difficulty” sliders.

03.1 Energy economy and ecological accounting

Track energy as a conserved or explicitly budgeted quantity. Feeding adds energy from finite resource stock; movement, maintenance, growth, thermoregulation proxies, reproduction and damage consume it. If food regenerates, its production rate and carrying capacity must be explicit. Otherwise populations can grow without an ecological ceiling.

ΔEᵢ = intake(food, access, competition) − maintenance − movement − reproduction − damage

03.2 Predation as an evolved role, not a species flag

The same population can express predatory strategies if traits create conditional differences in attack, pursuit and prey choice. The phenotype should be inferred from behaviour and outcomes. A useful strategy space includes:

Offensive traits

Attack threshold, aggression, pursuit persistence, acceleration, burst speed, strike precision, jaw/weapon proxy, group coordination.

Detection traits

Visual range, field of view, motion sensitivity, chemical cue proxy, memory, target discrimination and sensory noise.

Defensive traits

Escape acceleration, turning agility, camouflage proxy, schooling response, vigilance, alarm signalling and refuge use.

Context traits

Hunger threshold, risk tolerance, energy reserves, injury cost, body-size asymmetry and kin-recognition bias.

Attack should be an expected-value decision, not an unconditional collision: probability of capture × energy gained minus pursuit cost, injury risk and lost foraging opportunity. Hunting a relative may carry a kin-selection cost if the model explicitly represents that information.

U(attack) = P(capture | traits, geometry) · reward − pursuit_cost − injury_risk − kin_cost
Preventing an accidental predator caste
Do not store a permanent predator/prey species bit. Store heritable continuous traits and transient state variables. An individual may attack when hungry and advantaged, flee when outmatched, scavenge when a carcass is available, and forage otherwise. Record attack attempts, pursuit duration, captures, failed attacks and energy return to reveal whether predation is actually viable.

03.3 Density dependence, niches and spatial ecology

Local resource depletion and spatially uneven competition can create patch use, dispersal, local extinctions and recolonisation. A global average resource level hides these effects. Track resource gradients, local density, encounter rates, refuge availability and movement between patches.

03.4 Life-history trade-offs

Traits that improve immediate survival may reduce reproduction or future survival. Body size can improve contest ability while increasing maintenance cost; early reproduction can increase current offspring count while reducing future reproduction; high activity can improve food acquisition but increase energy expenditure and detection risk.

Trade-offPotential benefitPotential costMeasure
Body sizeCombat advantage, prey range, reserves.Metabolic demand, slower turning, longer maturation.Lifetime reproductive success by size.
ActivityMore encounters and food.Higher energy use and exposure.Net energy gain per distance.
FecundityMore offspring per event.Higher parental investment, lower offspring survival.Recruitment per parent.
SocialityDetection, dilution, cooperative hunting.Competition, disease/contact cost, kin conflict.Fitness by group size and relatedness.
04 / EVOLUTIONARY DYNAMICS

Heritable variation, selection and coevolution

Evolution is change in inherited variation across generations. A population that changes behaviour during its lifetime is not necessarily evolving; learning and plasticity must be distinguished from genetic change.

04.1 Genotype, phenotype and inheritance

Keep genotype separate from expressed phenotype. A genotype encodes heritable parameters; phenotype is the result of genotype interacting with age, energy, environment, learning and state. This separation enables reaction norms and context-dependent strategies.

Mutation and recombination
Mutation introduces new heritable variants; recombination/crossover reshuffles existing variation. For numeric traits, consider bounded additive mutations with optional correlated pleiotropic effects. For neural genomes, preserve innovation identity so matching genes can be aligned during crossover. Avoid mutation rates so high that selection cannot retain useful combinations.
Genetic architecture
Start with explicit continuous loci for interpretable traits and a separate sparse neural genome for behavioural control. Record heritability, mutation rate, allele distributions, covariance and genotype-to-phenotype mapping. A gene count or weight average alone is not enough to describe diversity.

04.2 Selection, drift and population size

Selection arises when heritable variants differ in expected reproductive success. Drift is stochastic change due to finite sampling and can dominate in small populations. Migration and spatial structure change how variation spreads. These processes should be separately measurable.

ProcessMechanismExpected signatureControl / diagnostic
Directional selectionOne trait value yields higher reproductive success.Trait mean shifts.Fitness regression, selection differential.
Stabilising selectionIntermediate values outperform extremes.Variance contracts.Fitness curve across trait quantiles.
Disruptive selectionMultiple trait regions outperform intermediates.Multimodality may emerge.Trait distribution and local fitness.
Genetic driftRandom sampling of parents/offspring.Allele frequencies wander.Replicate runs at varied population sizes.
Gene flowDispersal and mating between patches.Spatial similarity changes.Relatedness and differentiation by location.

04.3 Mate choice, kin affinity and sexual selection

Mate preference is itself heritable and can coevolve with the traits being preferred. Candidate sampling, encounter rates, energetic state, compatibility and choice costs affect realised mating. Kin affinity is not a universal “goodness” slider: it can increase cooperation toward relatives while creating inbreeding costs if close relatives mate.

Mate score = encounter chance × preference(match) × eligibility − search cost − inbreeding penalty
Kin selection and inclusive fitness
Hamilton's rule is commonly expressed as rB > C, where relatedness-weighted benefit to recipients exceeds cost to the actor. It is a conceptual criterion under specified assumptions, not a magic coefficient to add to every social action. Track who benefits, how related they are and what the actor sacrifices.
Assortative and disassortative mating
Similarity preference can preserve local trait clusters; dissimilarity preference can increase heterozygosity or mix strategies. Neither is automatically adaptive. Measure offspring viability, diversity and long-term reproductive success rather than assuming one preference direction is superior.

04.4 Eco-evolutionary feedback and coevolution

Ecology changes selection pressures, while evolution changes ecological interactions. Spatially explicit models can show local extinction, recolonisation and temporary escape from selection. Population-average differential equations can conceal these local effects.

05 / NEURAL SYSTEMS

Neural controllers, neuroevolution and learning

Neural networks provide flexible mappings from perception and internal state to action. Their usefulness depends on sensor quality, reward design, inherited variation and whether evolved behaviour remains interpretable.

05.1 Controller architecture

A compact recurrent controller is appropriate for agents whose decisions depend on recent context. Inputs can include neighbour density, heading alignment, predator threat, food direction, mate proximity, energy reserve, age and memory state. Outputs should be bounded action requests, not direct edits to position.

05.2 NeuroEvolution of Augmenting Topologies (NEAT)

Stanley and Miikkulainen (2002) introduced NEAT, combining evolving network weights and topology. Its central ideas are innovation numbers for historical gene alignment, speciation to protect structural innovations, and incremental growth from minimal networks. It is not simply “randomly mutate the number of neurons.”

NEAT mechanisms
  • Innovation tracking: identify matching structural mutations across lineages for crossover.
  • Compatibility distance: group related genomes into species using excess, disjoint and weight-difference terms.
  • Speciation: protect new structures while they are initially weak.
  • Incremental complexification: start simple and add nodes/connections when mutation introduces them.

For a browser simulation, full canonical NEAT can be expensive and complex. A sparse fixed-topology controller with rewiring is easier to implement but should not be described as NEAT unless it implements the defining mechanisms. A staged path is: explicit innovation IDs → matching-gene crossover → compatibility distance → species niches → adaptive thresholds.

05.3 Learning within a lifetime

Plasticity changes connection weights during an individual's lifetime. It must be kept distinct from inherited mutation. If learned weights are not inherited, the next generation receives the genetic initialisation, not the acquired state. If they are inherited, specify the inheritance mechanism and test for runaway reward hacking.

MechanismTimescaleRoleRisk
EvolutionGenerationsChanges inherited policy architecture/weights.Slow search, drift, premature convergence.
PlasticityLifetimeAdapts response from experience.Reward hacking, unstable weights.
State / memorySeconds to minutesRetains recent sensory context.Hidden state can be hard to interpret.

05.4 Interpretability and behavioural phenotyping

Do not infer strategy from a single connection weight. Record input activation distributions, output activation distributions, hidden-state statistics, action frequencies, response latency and outcomes. Use controlled perturbations: present a threat from different bearings, remove food, vary neighbour density and compare action changes.

Critical safeguard: if reward is calculated from energy change, an agent may exploit side effects of the energy accounting rather than learn a robust strategy. Log reward components independently and run ablations with learning disabled.
06 / IMPLEMENTATION ARCHITECTURE

Turning the research into a robust browser simulation

The current project already has a worker-based architecture, typed-array state, spatial indexing, sparse inherited neural controllers and browser-local persistence. The priority is to make the causal model measurable and the runtime predictable, not to replace working infrastructure for sport.

06.1 Recommended simulation loop

Recommended ordering is not universal; for example, energy may be updated before or after movement depending on the chosen accounting convention. Define one consistent convention and test it. Birth/death mutation should occur at a controlled boundary so arrays and indexes remain valid.

06.2 Data model and state boundaries

State classExamplesPersistence
Heritable genotypeTrait loci, neural genes, mutation metadata.Saved and inherited.
Developed phenotypeBody size, capacity, sensory range.Recomputed or saved with versioning.

Versioned snapshots
Every saved world should include schema version, simulation version, seed/RNG state, config, population arrays, genomes, dynamic state, resources and bounded history. Migrations should be explicit and tested. Preserve a last-known-good snapshot and reject malformed arrays before restoring the worker.

06.3 Performance strategy

The repository uses a worker and typed arrays. Keep rendering separate from simulation and avoid allocating objects inside hot loops. Spatial hashing is a major win, but a dense cell can still become quadratic locally. Profile distributions of candidate count, not just total population.

Measure first

Step time, render time, neighbour candidates, average/max cell occupancy, neural evaluations, allocation/GC pauses, snapshot duration.

Bound work

Fixed timestep, capped catch-up, bounded history, sampling limits, adaptive render quality and explicit max candidate counts where scientifically acceptable.

Parallelise carefully

Worker owns mutable state. Consider WebGPU only after CPU profiles identify a stable data-parallel bottleneck.

Preserve observability

Performance degradation should not silently alter the model's biology. If population adaptation changes agent count, mark it as a simulation intervention.

06.4 Validation and reproducibility

Use deterministic seeds and unit tests for mathematical invariants. A pretty emergent pattern is not proof of correctness. Separate numerical validation, ecological plausibility and user-interface checks.

Trait mean/variance, heritability, covariance, genotype distance, allele frequency, effective population size and lineage survival.

Topology size, connection weights, output entropy, action frequencies, sensory response curves, plasticity magnitude and lifetime learning delta.

07.3 Implementation roadmap

Phase A · Scientific instrumentation
  • Define units and timestep conventions for motion, energy, age and reproduction.
  • Add deterministic seed control and event-level telemetry.
  • Expose perception and interaction geometry.
  • Build exportable time series and experiment metadata.
Phase B · Causal ecology
  • Audit every energy source and sink.
  • Represent attack attempts, pursuit, capture and costs as explicit events.
  • Remove hard-coded role assumptions where present; derive role from actions and traits.
  • Add spatial resource depletion and recovery measurements.
Phase C · Evolutionary integrity
  • Separate genotype, phenotype, plastic weights and transient state.
  • Measure selection differential, drift and effective population size.
  • Add explicit mutation/recombination tests.
  • Evaluate mate preference and kin effects with controlled relatedness.
Phase D · Neural evolution
  • Benchmark fixed steering against neural controllers under identical conditions.
  • Track structural innovation and crossover integrity.
  • Add speciation only if it demonstrably improves topology evolution.
  • Run plasticity ablations and reward-component audits.
Phase E · Physics depth and performance
  • Benchmark fixed-step integrators and collision correctness.
  • Profile dense spatial cells and worst-case local search.
  • Introduce coherent current fields and flow coupling.
  • Prototype SPH separately, with stability and conservation tests before integration.
First implementation priority: make the existing model observable and falsifiable. Add more complex mechanics only when telemetry shows what scientific question they answer.

Evidence and interpretation notes

What this report does not claim
  • It does not claim Reynolds' animation model is a complete theory of animal schooling.
  • It does not claim a simple energy-based fitness proxy equals biological fitness.
  • It does not claim a sparse rewiring network is canonical NEAT.
  • It does not claim an analytic current field or SPH particle effect is validated oceanography.
  • It does not claim predator-like behaviour proves a real trophic level or stable polymorphism.
  • It does not claim illustrative graphs contain measured Boids data.
How to extend the bibliography
For each new source, record full citation, DOI or stable URL, study type, model assumptions, key result, limitations, and exactly which design decision it informs. Prefer primary literature and systematic reviews over tutorial summaries. Mark preprints and non-peer-reviewed sources explicitly.
